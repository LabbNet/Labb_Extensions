01

How the portal reaches them

There is no username and no password. The portal is one address — app.labbpo.com/me — and access arrives as a link in a text message.

Two ways the link goes out

  • Automatically. System messages — the getting-started text, selection notices, payment reminders — carry a one-tap sign-in link.
  • On demand, from you. Use this when someone lost the text or the link expired.
Offenders list
The portal icon on the person’s row — “Text/email a one-tap link to the offender’s portal.”
Dashboard
Send Link on the person’s card.
Clinic roster
Send Portal Link on the roster row.
Requirement
A phone number or email on file. Without one, the button returns “No phone or email on file for this offender. Add one first.”
Record kept
Each send is written to the audit log as send_portal_link, attributed to you.
Why it matters

The address never changes, so “I lost the link” is never a re-enrollment. Send a new one and they are back in — same account, same history.

02

Signing in

Possession of the phone is the credential. Either they tap the link in the text, or they type in their number and receive a code.

Enter your phone number and we’ll text you a code to sign in.

(555) 123-4567

The fallback screen when there’s no live link to tap.

One tap — the link in the text

The link looks like app.labbpo.com/m/K7v2Qop. Tapping it signs them straight in with nothing to type. The code inside it is good for 30 minutes and works more than once inside that window, so re-tapping the same text still works for a while. After that it expires and they see: “Your sign-in link has expired. Enter your phone number and we’ll send a new way in.”

Phone plus code

They enter their number and tap Send me a code. The reply is always the same sentence — “If that number is on file, a code is on its way” — whether or not the number belongs to anyone. The screen will not confirm that a person is in the program.

If they say the code never arrives

Check the phone number on their record first. The screen says the same thing for a number that is on file and one that isn’t, so a typo in the record looks identical to a carrier delay.

More than one person uses this number. Who are you?

Code entry, and the picker shown when a phone is shared.

The session is short on purpose

A successful sign-in lasts 30 minutes. Nothing is stored on the phone afterward — a borrowed or lost handset does not become a standing key to someone’s record.

Shared phones

When two people on the same caseload share a number, the code lands once and the portal asks “More than one person uses this number. Who are you?” Names appear as first name plus last initial. Choosing one signs into that account only — the other person’s record is not visible.

Limits

Three code requests per phone number and ten per network address in any fifteen minutes. Past that they see “Too many attempts. Please wait a few minutes and try again.” Repeated wrong codes pause the attempts the same way.

Your name is on it

Before they agree to anything, the welcome screen names your office and you. That is the answer to the first question most people have about an unexpected text — who is this from, and is it real.

What it promises

  • See your test status — whether there’s a test today, and where to go.
  • Private and secure — information goes to your office, nowhere else.
  • All in one place — terms, payment, notification settings.
  • Helpful reminders — so a requirement isn’t missed.

Then a three-step path: agree to terms, add a payment method, done. The tone is deliberately supportive rather than punitive — the goal of this screen is that someone finishes setup on the first sitting instead of putting the phone down.

The one other control is “This isn’t me — sign out,” for a wrong number or a shared handset.

04

The terms gate

Until they tap I Agree, the rest of the portal doesn’t open. Not the dashboard, not today’s test, not their PIN.

⚠ Action needed: Terms of Service

Monthly monitoring fee: $35.00

Text message consent: You agree to receive SMS messages about your monitoring program (compliance, appointment, and account notices). Message & data rates may apply. Reply STOP at any time to unsubscribe.

By tapping “I Agree” you accept the monitoring Terms of Service and consent to receive text-message notifications.

Sign out

The only screen available until terms are accepted.

What they read

  • The monthly monitoring fee, in dollars.
  • Any clauses your office added to its terms.
  • The text-message consent paragraph, including “Reply STOP at any time.”

One tap records both the terms agreement and the SMS consent — there is no second form to chase.

The single most common support call

“I signed in but there’s nothing there.” Nine times out of ten they are sitting on this screen and haven’t scrolled to I Agree. The moment they tap it, the full dashboard appears.

“Here is how it works” comes first

Your Details

Their name, your name, the office, and the assigned testing site with address. Get Directions opens the native Maps app — by coordinates when the site has them, otherwise by address. Anything unassigned reads “Not yet assigned,” which is a useful prompt back to you.

Compliance status

Green In Compliance means exactly two things are true: terms accepted and a card on file. Red Out of Compliance names what’s missing and scrolls straight to the fix when tapped.

Read this one carefully

“In Compliance” on this banner is about account setup — terms and a payment method. It says nothing about test results or attendance. Someone with a positive result still sees a green banner, and that is not a system error.

Free-month incentive

If your account runs the promotion, a banner counts down the ten-day window: add a card, make the first payment, and accept terms by the date shown, and the next month is free. Once earned it turns into “You earned a free month.”

Today’s Test

Driven by the same selection you make. When one exists: “You have a test scheduled today,” the site, and directions. When not: “No test scheduled today — check back here each day.”

Talk to Your Officer

The video-conference button is visible but not live. Tapping it says “This feature is not active for your account,” and the tap is counted so the product team can see real demand. Tell people it’s coming, not broken.

Where the card actually goes

Adding or updating a card opens Stripe’s own page in a new tab. LabbPO only ever receives the brand and last four digits. That line — “your card is encrypted and never stored on our servers” — is accurate, and it is worth repeating to someone who is hesitant.

Fee schedule

The monthly subscription rate set by your office is the only charge an offender carries. The old missed-test, no-card, and reinstatement penalties were removed from the offender flow — if someone insists they were charged a penalty, they weren’t.

Result history, notification choices, and the two bottom actions.

Result History

Every result reported to you, in their own portal, labelled plainly: Negative in green, Positive and Missed Test (No-Show) in red, Result Pending in amber. Positive panels list the substances as chips.

Each row carries Comment or Dispute. They pick one of the two, write up to a few paragraphs, and submit. Their row then shows what they filed, the date, and whether it is open or resolved.

This lands in your inbox

A dispute or comment emails you immediately with the full text — subject line “Marcus Reyes disputed a test result” — and is recorded against the result. Nothing about the result itself changes; the record simply now carries their side of it.

Notifications

Five channels, and they may pick more than one:

  • Text (SMS) — a text when they have a test.
  • Email — the same, by email.
  • Call-In — they call the number and enter their PIN.
  • Text-In — they text their PIN to check status.
  • Portal Only — no texts or emails at all.

Choosing Portal Only switches off the push channels and warns them in writing: “with Portal Only we won’t text or email you. You’ll need to check this portal each day to see if you have a test.”

Worth knowing before a violation hearing

Portal Only is the offender’s own choice, recorded in their preferences. “I never got a text” and “I turned texts off” are different facts, and the portal keeps both — every sign-in is logged along with whether a test was showing that day.

The two bottom actions

Sign out ends the session. Withdraw from program opens a confirmation that states plainly it notifies you and may be treated as a compliance violation, offers an optional reason box, and — on confirm — emails you. It does not deactivate anyone. That decision stays yours.

Screens that replace the dashboard

Two situations swap the whole portal for a single screen. In both, the person can still sign in — nobody is ever shut out of reading their own record.

Day 30 without a valid payment method.

Locked for non-payment

The clock runs from the day they entered the system, or from a declined card, or from the date you last unlocked them. Warnings go out by text at day 20, day 28, and day 29, each copied to you by email.

At day 30, three things happen at once:

  • The portal collapses to the screen shown here — nothing else is reachable.
  • A violation note is written on their profile, authored by the system.
  • They are removed from random selection and testing.

There is no reinstatement fee. Adding a valid card restores the account automatically, and their normal monthly rate resumes. An account owner can also unlock someone directly, which restarts a fresh 30-day window.

Accounts belonging to a deactivated officer, office, or account are skipped entirely — a closed office can’t generate lockouts.

Call Officer
Your account is deactivated

Marcus, your monitoring account with Weber County Adult Probation is no longer active. You have no tests or payments due. If you believe this is a mistake, contact your probation officer (Officer D. Whitfield) at 801-555-0188.

After you deactivate someone.

Deactivated

Someone you deactivate keeps their sign-in. They see a clear notice naming your office, you, and your phone number, and they are told plainly that no tests or payments are due. Every action flow — terms, payment, today’s test — is gone.

Design principle

Access to the portal is never used as a penalty. A deactivated or delinquent person can still sign in and read what the system holds about them; what changes is what they can do.

07

What comes back to you

Most of what happens in the portal is silent. Four things aren’t.

Email

A dispute or comment

Arrives with their full text and the result it refers to. Subject: “[Name] disputed a test result.” The result itself is unchanged — review it on their profile.

Email

A withdrawal request

Their case number, phone, email, office, and any reason they gave. They are not deactivated — the request is recorded and handed to you.

Audit log

Every portal view

Each time they open the portal it is logged, including whether a test was showing that day. This is the record behind “I was never notified.”

Audit log

Terms and consent

The terms agreement and SMS consent are timestamped when they tap I Agree, alongside every link you send.
